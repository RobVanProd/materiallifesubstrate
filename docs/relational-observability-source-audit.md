# Relational observability source audit

## Accepted implementation inheritance

The Candidate-C row is inherited unchanged from the accepted Mechanical
Observability Lab. It is the Jacobian of physical center distance, assembled
as `[-n_ij,+n_ij]` after canonical packet/relation validation. It rejects
coincident endpoints and all nonfinite coordinate/norm cases. It contains no
force, stiffness, material tag, grid gradient, or persistent numerical mode.

The exact inherited blob hashes are frozen in the preregistration. The
accepted parent evidence contains 59 Candidate-C configurations and 7,646
relation rows. An independent pre-implementation audit found raw row norms in
`[1.4142135623730945,1.4142135623730956]`; the new run turns that observation
into an explicit per-row gate.

## Prior-art boundary

Central-distance rigidity matrices are standard bar-and-joint kinematics:
their kernel contains infinitesimal translations and rotations, while extra
kernel vectors are mechanisms/floppy modes. Classical bond-based peridynamics
also uses objective central pair information, but its particular force law is
not imported here. Corrected SPH/TLSPH and correspondence gradients remain
prior-art diagnostics only; their known zero-energy/hourglass issues are why
affine reproduction alone was not accepted as observability evidence.

No primary-paper equation is newly implemented in this branch: the selected
operator is the already audited derivative of Euclidean distance. Literature
claims do not enter the decision gate. The lab asks only whether the explicit
finite relation graph carries a resolved rigidity quotient under the frozen
configuration matrix.

## Separation from the accepted negative/control evidence

The sealed Mechanical Observability and Kelvin Covariance bundles remain
unchanged. The inherited fixture exporter is used only to reproduce packet
coordinates and Candidate-C topology under byte-checked table hashes. The new
producer does not parse Candidate-B operator/rank output, and it never parses
Candidate-D volume rows. Thus neither the Candidate-B diagnostic-coordinate
defect nor any Candidate-D result can select Candidate C.
